Fractal impedance control

The control law at the centre of this framework. See Control Modes and H-PMP.

ReferenceRelevance
K. K. Babarahmati, C. Tiseo, J. Smith, H.-C. Lin, M. S. Erden and M. Mistry, “Fractal impedance for passive controllers: a framework for interaction robotics,” Nonlinear Dynamics, vol. 110, no. 3, pp. 2517–2533, 2022. doi:10.1007/s11071-022-07754-3 The FIC formulation itself — the passive, energy-bounded controller every mode in this Studio runs.
C. Tiseo et al., “HapFIC: An Adaptive Force/Position Controller for Safe Environment Interaction in Articulated Systems,” IEEE Trans. Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering, vol. 29, pp. 1432–1440, 2021. doi:10.1109/TNSRE.2021.3098062 The bounded admittance implemented as Joint HAP-FIC (mode 4), Cartesian HAP-FIC (mode 8) and the joint layer of H-PMP.
N. Hogan, “Impedance Control: An Approach to Manipulation,” ASME J. Dynamic Systems, Measurement, and Control, 1985. The origin of impedance control; the baseline every later scheme, FIC included, is measured against.

Contact localization from proprioception

Directly relevant to Contact Localization. Both of the first two entries do more than this Studio's feature does — multi-contact, point-level — but both require a base force/torque sensor, which the Kinova Gen3 here does not have. That asymmetry is the whole reason our claim is restricted to link identity.

ReferenceRelevance
S. W. Han and M. J. Kim, “Proprioceptive Sensor-Based Simultaneous Multi-Contact Point Localization and Force Identification for Robotic Arms,” IEEE ICRA, pp. 12099–12105, 2023. IEEE Xplore · arXiv:2303.03903 Particle filter over preprocessed link meshes, joint torque sensors plus a base F/T sensor. 0.16 cm single-contact RMSE — but simulation only. Source of the observation that multi-contact “is challenging when the robot is equipped with only JTSs.”
“CDM: Contact Diffusion Model for Multi-Contact Point Localization,” 2025. arXiv:2502.06109 Diffusion model on joint torque sensors plus an ATI Mini85 base F/T sensor, validated on real hardware, on a Kinova Gen3: 0.44 cm single-contact, 1.24 cm dual. The closest published work to our setup, and the reason our contribution is framed as a feature rather than a result.
P. Manuelli and R. Tedrake, “Localizing external contact using proprioceptive sensors: The contact particle filter,” IEEE/RSJ IROS, 2016. The contact particle filter that most later proprioceptive localization work descends from.
A. De Luca and R. Mattone, “Sensorless robot collision detection and hybrid force/motion control,” IEEE ICRA, 2005. The generalized momentum observer — the standard way to estimate external joint torque without an F/T sensor. Our estimator uses a simpler quasi-static gravity residual instead, which is valid only because the arm holds still.
S. Haddadin, A. De Luca and A. Albu-Schäffer, “Robot Collisions: A Survey on Detection, Isolation, and Identification,” IEEE Trans. Robotics, vol. 33, no. 6, pp. 1292–1312, 2017. The detection / isolation / identification taxonomy. This feature performs isolation (which link), not identification (what force, where exactly).
A. Bicchi, J. K. Salisbury and D. L. Brock, “Contact Sensing from Force Measurements,” Int. J. Robotics Research, 1993. Intrinsic contact sensing, and the original statement of what a force measurement can and cannot tell you about contact location.

Real-time control and the inner-loop MCU

Relevant to Inner-Loop MCU. These are also the references of the inner-loop technical report.

Y. Maruyama, S. Kato and T. Azumi, “Exploring the performance of ROS2,” EMSOFT, 2016. doi:10.1145/2968478.2968502 ROS 2 latency and jitter: why the 1 kHz joint loop should not depend on the middleware path.

B. Hannaford and J.-H. Ryu, “Time-domain passivity control of haptic interfaces,” IEEE Trans. Robotics and Automation, 2002. doi:10.1109/70.988969 Passivity of haptic loops under delay.
