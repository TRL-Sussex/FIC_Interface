Hapfic — Hybrid Cartesian+Joint Control

A novel two-stage control architecture that combines Cartesian task-space planning with per-joint haptic admittance, enabling compliant teleoperation with Cartesian trajectory shaping.

Novel contribution

Hapfic is one of the key algorithmic contributions of the FIC framework. It solves the fundamental tension in robot teleoperation: Cartesian planners give intuitive task-space control but are stiff; joint admittance gives compliance but lacks Cartesian trajectory shaping. Hapfic fuses both — the outer loop thinks in Cartesian space while the inner loop remains compliant at each joint.

What problem does Hapfic solve?

In standard Cartesian control modes (5–8), the controller either:

  • Impedance (Mode 5): Directly maps Cartesian error to joint torques via the Jacobian transpose. Fast but no trajectory shaping.
  • Planner (Mode 6): Smooths the Cartesian trajectory with acceleration limits and damping. Smooth but stiff — the robot tracks the plan rigidly.
  • Admittance (Modes 7–8): The robot yields to external forces. Compliant but operates purely in Cartesian space — the admittance target feeds the Cartesian planner and impedance, with no per-joint compliance layer.

None of these modes give you both smooth Cartesian trajectory planning and per-joint compliant response to interaction forces. That combination is exactly what you need for safe, intuitive teleoperation — the operator commands in Cartesian space (natural), the trajectory is shaped smoothly (safe), and the robot yields compliantly at each joint when it contacts the environment (reactive).

Two-stage architecture

Hapfic achieves this through a two-stage pipeline (cartesianHapfic() in fic_control.h) executed every control cycle (1 kHz):

Stage 1 — Cartesian planner + impedance (task space)

  1. Planned position: The Cartesian target Xt is fed through FIC planners (channels 16–18 for XYZ) that smooth the trajectory with acceleration limits and damping. Output: planned EE position pos_plan.
  2. Translational wrench: For each axis, a FIC controller (channels 16–18) maps the position error pos_plan - curr_pos through the nonlinear saturation curve to produce a force command.
  3. Orientation wrench: Quaternion error between desired and current orientation is computed as q_err = q_des * q_curr.conjugate(). The scalar angle θ = 2 * atan(||q_v|| / max(q_s, 1e-12)) feeds through a FIC controller (channel 19). The resulting torque is projected along the error axis with a sign correction for quaternion double-cover.
  4. Joint torques: The full 6D wrench (3 forces + 3 torques) maps to joint torques via τdes = JT * wrench + g(q).

Note that Stage 1 never reads the end-effector force/torque sensor — the wrench argument passed into cartesianHapfic() is unused. All compliance comes from Stage 2's per-joint torque comparison.

Stage 2 — Per-joint haptic admittance (joint space)

  1. Each joint receives τdes[i] from Stage 1 as its commanded effort.
  2. The joint's haptic admittance controller (handleJointAdmitanceControl with mode 2, as in Mode 4 Joint Admittance Haptic) is driven by the interaction torque alone, τdes[i] - τmeasured[i], split into the part that pushes on the end-effector (deadband in task space) and the rest (per-joint deadband; see Torque deadband below), and produces an admittance offset adm_delta, bounded to the joint law's outer breakpoint ±xb,n.
  3. The offset is added to the joint's base target, the IK-derived joint solution q_target_ik[i] (an iterative IK, Pinocchio, solves the Cartesian target into joint space whenever a new EE target arrives), limited to ±xb,n (at least 0.1 rad) about the joint's measured position, so the offset can cancel it: q_ref[i] = limit(q_target_ik[i]) + adm_delta (clamped to joint limits). A blocked end-effector therefore does not wind up the joint errors.
  4. The compliant target feeds the joint's FIC planner and then the joint impedance loop (handleJointImpedanceControlWithPlanner), and the node adds g(q). On the MCU path the host applies the set-point limit.
Why this works

Stage 1 "thinks" in Cartesian space — it plans a smooth trajectory and computes the wrench needed to track it. But instead of directly commanding those torques, it suggests them to the per-joint admittance layer. If the environment pushes a joint (measured torque ≠ desired torque), that joint yields compliantly — without disturbing the Cartesian planning. With nothing pushing, the gap is Stage 1's own torque, so the offsets move the arm back towards the goal until that torque is inside the deadband; at a contact a joint starts yielding when its gap reaches the deadband b and keeps yielding until the gap is below b/4, so a held contact ends with each joint within b/4 of the desired torque. The result is a robot that follows Cartesian commands smoothly, yet is soft at every joint when touched.

FIC channel layout

Hapfic uses dedicated FIC controller channels to avoid collisions with the standard joint and null-space controllers:

ChannelsOwnerPurpose
0 .. n_j-1Joint controllersHapfic inner loop (Stage 2) — per-joint admittance + planner + impedance
6 .. 8Elbow null-space (standard Cartesian modes)Not used under Hapfic: Hapfic moves the elbow to its own channels 20..22
9 .. 9+n_j-1Postural null-spaceIndependent — can run alongside Hapfic
16 .. 18Hapfic Cartesian (XYZ)Stage 1 — planner + impedance for translation
19Hapfic Cartesian (rot)Stage 1 — impedance for orientation (scalar angle)
20 .. 22Elbow null-space (under Hapfic)Elbow helper X/Y/Z, initialised by initHapficCartesian()

The offset of 16 is set by hapfic_cart_planner_offset_ and ensures that Hapfic's Cartesian channels never overlap with joint controllers (0..6 for a 7-DOF robot), elbow (6..8 in the standard Cartesian modes), or postural (9..15). The FIC instance has 23 channels.

The elbow has its own channels under Hapfic

In the standard Cartesian modes the elbow helper's FIC controllers use channels 6..8. Under Hapfic, Stage 2 runs joint channels 0..n_j-1, which on a 7-DOF robot (Franka FR3, Kinova Gen3) include 6, so initHapficCartesian() initialises the elbow at channels 20..22 (hapfic_cart_planner_offset_ + 4) and both nodes pass fic_ctrl_.elbowOffset() to applyElbowControl. Earlier versions shared channel 6 between joint 6 and elbow X and never initialised the elbow under Hapfic.

Initialization

When the Hapfic toggle changes, the enable callback sets initControllerPLanner = false, forcing a full controller re-initialization on the next control cycle; a message repeating the current value is ignored. Initialization is gated on hapfic_enabled_ && (mode == 6 || mode == 7 || mode == 8) and uses:

ComponentParameter sourceRationale
Joint controllers (0..n_j-1)HAP-FIC block, joint layer (<joint>)The inner loop runs joint haptic admittance — these parameters set its controller, planner, and admittance blocks
Cartesian planner+impedance (16..19)HAP-FIC block, Cartesian layer (<cartesian>)Stage 1 needs the Cartesian planner's acceleration limits and the impedance stiffness curve

The HAP-FIC block is <mode id="100" name="HAP-FIC"> in config.xml (100 is not a control mode). A robot without one runs Hapfic on Mode 4's joints and Mode 6's Cartesian block, and the node logs which tuning Hapfic is on.

Since v4.4.0 the parameters are not read at this point. The control loop asks for the tuning set fic::tuningKeyFor gives for the mode (fic::TuningKey): with Hapfic on in Modes 6–8 both layers are keyed to the HAP-FIC block, so toggling Hapfic changes the key and the set is read once in the background (fic::loadTuningSet), with the robot's <postural> block in the same read, and the loop sends no new command until it is in memory. Toggling back to a key already read reads it again. See config.xml.

Initialization code path (identical structure in fic_simulation.cpp and fic_hardware.cpp):

// Tuning set for this mode (Hapfic on: both layers from the HAP-FIC block, mode 100),
// read off the control thread; nothing runs until it is in memory
const fic::TuningKey tuning_want = fic::tuningKeyFor(robot, n_j, mode, hapfic_enabled);
if (!refreshTuning(tuning_want, active_profile)) return;

// Init joint controllers, planners, and admittance at channels 0..n_j-1
initJointControllers(n_j, q_current, joint_scalars_, ...);

// Init Hapfic Cartesian at channels 16..19, and the elbow helper at 20..22
fic_ctrl_.initHapficCartesian(cart_params_, ee_curr_vec);

// Postural helper at channels 9..9+n_j-1, from the robot's <postural> block
fic_ctrl_.postural.init(n_j, postural_scalars_, postural_lists_);

Compatible modes

Hapfic engages on Cartesian modes 6, 7, and 8. When active, it replaces the standard Cartesian control law with the two-stage pipeline:

Base modeWithout HapficWith Hapfic
6 — Cartesian PlannerRC.planner()RC.hapfic()
7 — Cartesian AdmittanceRC.admittance(m=1)RC.hapfic()
8 — Cartesian Admittance HapticRC.admittance(m=2)RC.hapfic()

The base mode decides what happens to the Cartesian goal before Stage 1 (since v5.3.0); the joint layer is the same on all three:

Base modeA push on the armWhen the push ends
6the joints yield; the goal stays as commandedthe task layer draws the arm back to the commanded goal
7 (M1)the joints yield and the goal follows the push, up to ±30 cm / ±30°the goal stays where the push left it: the arm holds its new pose (whole-arm hand-guiding)
8 (M2)the joints yield and the goal gives way up to ±5 cm / ±5°; beyond that the Cartesian law resiststhe goal stays at its shifted position and the arm settles there

On modes 7 and 8 this is the host mode's own task-space admittance (hapficTaskAdmittance, step for step the one of RC.admittance()), driven by the estimated external wrench alone with the 4 N / 0.2 N·m deadbands, but on Hapfic's own integrators (the admittance blocks of channels 16–21) and state, with the admittance parameters of the HAP-FIC block's Cartesian layer. The IK set-point follows the shifted goal to first order, q_ik + J# Δx (damped pseudo-inverse, λ = 0.01), also on the inner-loop MCU path. On the robot the wrench is the joint-torque estimate pinv(JT)(τ̂ − g); in simulation it comes from the joint torques MuJoCo's contacts apply (contact_torques), because there the measured torque is the controller's own command and the estimate would read the arm's own effort as a push.

The Stage 2 admittance always runs in haptic mode (m=2, as Mode 4): the offset is driven by the gap τdes − τmeas alone and bounded to ±xb,n; it has no spring of its own.

Mode 5 (Cartesian Impedance)

Mode 5 does not activate Hapfic in the execution path, though the toggle is visible on all Cartesian modes (5–8). The init and execution checks gate on modes 6, 7, and 8. Mode 5 uses direct impedance with no planner — Hapfic's Stage 1 planner would conflict with the pure impedance paradigm.

Orientation math

The orientation error computation deserves special attention as it handles the quaternion double-cover problem:

// Quaternion error: q_err = q_des * q_curr.conjugate()
Eigen::Quaterniond q_err = q_des * q_curr.conjugate();
Eigen::Vector3d q_v = q_err.vec();    // imaginary part (rotation axis * sin(θ/2))
double q_s = q_err.w();               // real part (cos(θ/2))
double vnorm = q_v.norm();

// Rotation angle via atan (not atan2 — intentional)
double theta = 2.0 * std::atan(vnorm / std::max(q_s, 1e-12));

// FIC controller (channel 19) produces torque magnitude from angle error
double tau_cq = fic.control(hapfic_cart_planner_offset_ + 3, theta);

// Project torque along rotation axis, with sign correction for double-cover
vnorm = std::max(vnorm, 1e-12);   // guard against zero-error division
Eigen::Vector3d ori_torque = (q_s >= 0.0 ? 1.0 : -1.0) * tau_cq * (q_v / vnorm);

The sign(q_s) factor is critical: quaternions q and -q represent the same rotation, and without this correction the controller can produce torque in the wrong direction when the quaternion representation flips hemisphere.

Telemetry output

Both controller nodes publish the trajectory diagnostics on {robot_id}_trajectory_data (std_msgs/Float64MultiArray). The message begins with a per-joint block of 8 arrays of length n_j; the Cartesian diagnostic block starts at base = n_j * 8. Hapfic writes the fields from base + 3 onward (slots base + 0..2 belong to the standard admittance path):

Index (base = n_j × 8)DataDescription
base + 3..5curr_posCurrent EE position (X, Y, Z)
base + 6..8pos_plan - curr_posPosition error after planning
base + 9..11pos_planPlanned EE position
base + 12..14ori_torqueOrientation torque vector (3D)
base + 15thetaOrientation error angle (rad)

Stage 2 also fills the standard per-joint diagnostic slots: i + 5*n_j = compliant joint target q_ref[i], i + 6*n_j = desired effort τdes[i], i + 7*n_j = measured effort. These fields are available in the plotter for real-time visualization of the Cartesian planning trajectory vs. actual EE position.

Enabling Hapfic

Via the GUI

  1. Select a Cartesian control mode (6, 7, or 8) from the mode dropdown (see the table above for how they differ).
  2. In the Null-space row below the mode selector, check the Hapfic checkbox.
  3. The controller will re-initialize on the next control cycle with the Hapfic two-stage pipeline (watch for Hapfic ENABLED in the controller log).

The Hapfic checkbox is only visible when a Cartesian mode (5–8) is active. While enabled, the Dashboard shows a HAP badge in the active-modifiers indicator.

Tuning Hapfic

Hapfic tuning involves two independent parameter sets in the HAP-FIC block of config.xml (<mode id="100" name="HAP-FIC">), plus the per-robot deadband in robots_config.yaml:

Cartesian parameters (Stage 1)

Loaded from the HAP-FIC block's Cartesian layer (<cartesian>; Mode 6 on a robot without a HAP-FIC block) — axes <x>, <y>, <z> for translation and <q> for the orientation channel. Key elements:

ElementEffect
<controller><stiffness> (x/y/z)Cartesian stiffness — how strongly the EE tracks the planned position. Higher = stiffer tracking.
<controller><force_max>, <error_max>The nonlinear saturation curve — maximum force per error band.
<planner><damping_ratio>Planner damping — smoothness of trajectory transitions.
<planner><acc_max>, <vel_desired>Maximum Cartesian acceleration and desired velocity for the planner. Lower = smoother but slower response.
<controller><stiffness> (q)Orientation stiffness — angular tracking rigidity on channel 19.

Joint parameters (Stage 2)

Loaded from the HAP-FIC block's joint layer (<joint>; Mode 4 on a robot without a HAP-FIC block), per joint. Key elements:

ElementEffect
<controller><stiffness>Joint impedance stiffness — inner loop position tracking per joint.
<admittance><InvM>, <Damping>Admittance inverse mass and damping — how readily each joint yields to a torque mismatch.
<admittance><Flow>, <dFlow>, <Pose>Velocity, acceleration, and displacement limits on the admittance state.
Kinova Gen3: HAP-FIC profiles

With Mode 4's admittance damping (9.0 / 13.7) the KinovaGen3_7DOF Hapfic loop settles into a ~1 Hz oscillation. Its default HAP-FIC joint layer therefore has the admittance Damping at six times Mode 4's (54.0 / 82.2 / 54.0 / 96.9 / 54.0 / 54.0 / 54.0; the fourth joint keeps 96.9). The other profiles hold a HAP-FIC block only, so every other mode falls back to default:

  • sim: damping four times Mode 4's (36.0 / 54.8 / 36.0 / 96.9 / 36.0 / 36.0 / 36.0).
  • hw_x2, hw_x3, hw_x4, hw_x6: two to six times.
  • free_space (tasks without contact): admittance bound ±0.02 rad (every joint law compressed ten-fold in error, with its stiffness ×10), joint 4 able to yield (InvM 1), damping ten times Mode 4's.
  • contact (pressing and releasing): admittance bound ±0.4 rad, joint 4 InvM 0.1, damping as default.

Select one from the Config dropdown of the control bar (it publishes {rid}_active_profile).

Torque deadband (per robot)

The Stage 2 admittance ignores torque mismatches below a per-robot deadband, configured in fic_library/config/robots_config.yaml:

admittance_deadbands:
  joint_tau: 1.5     # Nm — minimum |τ_des − τ_meas| before a joint yields
  cart_force: 4.0    # N  — Cartesian deadband (standard admittance modes)
  cart_torque: 0.2   # Nm

Under Hapfic the joint deadband has hysteresis: a joint starts yielding when its gap reaches joint_tau and keeps yielding until the gap is below a quarter of it (0.5 N·m with the Kinova's 2.0), so a held contact ends within joint_tau/4 of the desired torque, while a bias below joint_tau is still ignored at rest. Too low a joint_tau makes the joints integrate torque-model bias and drift; too high makes the robot feel numb to light contact.

Split drive (v5.4.0). The gap vector u = τdes − τmeas is split into the part that produces an end-effector wrench, JTw with w = pinv(JT) u, and the rest. The first passes a deadband in task space on w (force and torque magnitudes, same hysteresis: engaged at the deadband, released below a quarter of it); the rest passes joint_tau per joint. A press therefore ends within a quarter of the task deadband of the Cartesian law at the end-effector, whatever the arm's lever arms; with the per-joint deadband alone it could stay several newtons above it. The task deadband is 1 N / 0.1 N·m in simulation and, on the robot, cart_force / cart_torque above (wide enough for the sensors' bias). The inner-loop MCU path keeps the per-joint deadband.

Tuning strategy

Start with the HAP-FIC block's default joint and Cartesian layers. To make the robot more compliant, raise the admittance InvM or lower Damping in the joint layer. To make Cartesian tracking tighter, raise the controller stiffness/force_max in the Cartesian layer. The Cartesian planner's damping_ratio and acc_max control how smoothly the EE transitions between targets — critical for teleoperation feel. Edit values live from the Tuning page (select HAP-FIC at the end of the Mode list, then the Joint layer or the Cartesian layer).

Teleoperation with contactOperator commands Cartesian targets (intuitive); robot yields at joints on contact (safe). Best of both worlds.
