MuJoCo Playground

A sandbox for learning to program the robot: an arm, a table and a few objects in the simulator, started in one click, then move it, record waypoints, play sequences, trace trajectories and pick objects.

Start

  1. Turn on MuJoCo Playground and Sequences in Settings → Extensions (both are on in the Docker image).
  2. Open Programming › MuJoCo Playground. Pick a Robot and a Scene: Pick and place (two cubes), Stacking (three cubes), Shapes (a cube, a ball and a can) or Empty table.
  3. Press Start. The Studio's own simulator and FIC controller start with the scene, exactly as its SIM and FIC buttons would, so everything else in the Studio works on it. Stop stops both and goes back to the robot's own scene.

The robot list comes from robots_config.yaml: any fixed-base arm whose simulator scene has the torque motors the FIC controller drives (torq_j1…). A robot that cannot be used says why (IIWA 14's scene has position servos; Spot is not an arm). Picking needs a gripper in simulation: the Kinova Gen3 has its Robotiq 2F-85, the Franka FR3's scene has none.

Start never touches a robot running its real arm: it refuses while that robot's controller runs on Hardware.

Simulate like the real robot

Off (the default), the Playground runs the ideal simulator, so the arm reaches its targets briskly. On, it runs the robot's hardware_model (Simulation vs Hardware): joint friction, damping and sensor noise. The arm then reaches targets more slowly and stops a little short of them, as the real one does.

The scene

A table stands in front of the robot (its top 0.2 m up; switch it off to put objects on the floor). Each object has a name, a shape (box, sphere, cylinder), a size, a position (x, y in the base frame) and a colour; it is placed resting on the table or the floor. Edit, add or remove objects while the Playground is stopped; the scene is remembered.

The scene file is written to ~/.fic_studio/playground/<robot>_playground.xml: the robot's own scene with the table and objects added (built with MuJoCo's model editor, mujoco.MjSpec), keeping every joint and actuator name, so the robot's tuning, gripper and hardware model apply as usual.

Where things are lists each object's live position: the simulator publishes the poses of the bodies named obj_* on robot<N>_simulation/object_poses (JSON, 10 Hz).

Exercises

  1. Move the arm. Open Joint or Cart in the control bar and drag a slider; Gripper opens and closes the hand.
  2. Record waypoints. In Quick Actions type a name and press New, then move the arm and press Snapshot → Capture for each point.
  3. Play it. Quick Actions plays, steps through (Step) or loops the sequence; Speed scales its hold times. The Sequences page edits it. In simulation a sequence switches the robot to a mode that follows each waypoint (Joint Impedance with Planner for joint waypoints, Cartesian Impedance with Planner for Cartesian ones).
  4. Trajectories. Quick Actions → Trajectory traces a line, a circle or a figure-eight from where the end effector is, in the plane you choose, holding its orientation: a stream of targets at 60 Hz that the controller follows as it goes. Save as sequence stores one cycle as waypoints instead: play both and compare a trajectory (followed continuously) with a sequence (each point reached, then the next). A larger or faster path lags further behind its target.
  5. Pick an object. Pick (on the page beside each object, or in Quick Actions) builds the pick as the sequence pick_<object> and plays it: home, open, above the object with the gripper pointing down, down to it, close, lift. Open it on the Sequences page to see and change every step.
  6. Script it. The Script Editor drives the same arm from Python; its examples move the joints and trace a circle.

Notes

  • The pick aims the grasp 20 mm into the object, as one does with a compliant controller: an impedance-controlled arm stops a little short of a target, and if it does get there the fingers stop on the table. Its grasp waypoint counts as reached within 45 mm (a waypoint may carry its own tolerance_mm and timeout_s).
  • Trajectories are limited to 300 mm/s at their fastest point in simulation, and to 50 mm/s, after a confirmation, when Sequences sends to a real robot (a real robot is never switched to another mode for it).
  • Extensions can add their own rows to Quick Actions (ExtensionHost.add_quick_action, see Extensions); the Playground's Pick is one.
